*A note. Short on purpose.*

## The shift

> The instrument has evolved. The responsibility to say something has not.

For most of the history of making things, fluency was a filter. To publish a clear paragraph, a competent image, a working piece of software, you had to acquire a craft, and the craft took years. The years kept a lot of empty work from ever being finished.

That filter is largely gone. Fluency is now available on demand, in most media, to anyone. This is genuinely good — it removes a barrier that had little to do with whether someone had something worth saying. But it also removes the accidental protection the barrier provided. A great deal of fluent, competent, empty work can now be finished and shipped.

## What did not change

Having something to say is still hard. It still requires paying attention to something long enough to form a view, being willing to be wrong in public, and caring whether the thing you make is true or worth someone's time.

None of that was automated. The instrument can render your point beautifully. It cannot supply the point, and it cannot make you mean it.

## The practical version

Before you use the instrument to say something, be able to answer:

- What am I actually claiming?
- Why do I believe it?
- Who is this for, and what do I want to be different after they have read it?
- Would I stand behind this sentence if my name were the only one on it?

If those answers are thin, a more powerful instrument will only help you produce a more convincing version of nothing.
