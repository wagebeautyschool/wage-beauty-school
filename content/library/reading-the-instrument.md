*A short checklist. Media literacy is not suspicion of everything; it is the habit of asking a few questions before a piece of media does its work on you.*

## The questions

**What made this, and with what?** A phone, a studio, a render farm, a language model, a committee. The means of production leaves fingerprints — in the framing, the pacing, the smoothness, the things left out.

**Who aimed it?** Media rarely drifts to you by accident. Someone chose the channel, the moment, the audience. Ask who benefits if you believe it, share it, or feel the way it wants you to feel.

**What does it want from you?** Attention, money, a vote, an emotion, a click, your silence. Name the ask. It is usually not hidden well once you look for it.

**What would have to be true for this to be fair?** Run the charitable version. If the piece is a fair representation, what does the world look like? Then check whether that world is the one you are in.

**What is the shape of what is missing?** Every frame has an edge. Every argument has a step it skips. The omission is often more informative than anything included.

## On generated media specifically

A generated artifact has no single author standing behind it and no single moment it was captured. It is an average of a great deal of prior material, shaped by a prompt you usually cannot see. The questions above still apply, but the answers get stranger: "who aimed it" might be a person with a one-line instruction, and "what is missing" might be everything the training data underrepresented.

Treat fluency as a neutral fact, not as evidence. The instrument is very good at sounding right. That is the thing it was built to do.
