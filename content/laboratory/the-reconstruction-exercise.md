## Question

Does reconstructing the decision history of an admired work improve the decisions you make in your own?

## Input

Five finished works chosen for admiration and variety: an essay, a short film scene, an open-source library, a song, and a small building.

## Method

For each, write a detailed speculative history: first version, likely cuts, genuine forks, dominant constraint, the thing the maker had to decide to say. Then, over the following weeks, keep a note whenever a decision in my own work seems to echo something from a reconstruction.

## Tools

A notebook. The works themselves. Where available, interviews or making-of material — read *after* the reconstruction, never before, so the guess stays honest.

## Process

Prototype stage. Four of five reconstructions done; the tracking of transfer into my own work has been running for about three weeks.

## Result

Preliminary and soft. Two concrete carry-overs so far:

- From the essay reconstruction: a habit of asking "what is the one sentence this exists to deliver" before drafting, which came directly from guessing at the essay's cut material.
- From the library reconstruction: more willingness to ship a smaller surface area, having spent an hour guessing at everything its author chose *not* to build.

## Observations

- Reading the making-of material afterwards was humbling. My reconstructions were plausible and frequently wrong about specifics, but usually right about the *shape* — where the hard forks were.
- The exercise is more engaging than analysing the finished object. It feels like sitting next to the maker rather than in front of the work.

## Failure points

- "Seems to echo" is not measurement. I am pattern-matching my own behaviour, which is exactly the kind of thing that produces false positives.
- Small sample, short window, no baseline for how many good decisions I would have made anyway.
- Selection bias in which works I chose to reconstruct.

## Next question

Is the value in the reconstruction itself, or just in spending an hour of concentrated attention on good work? A version of the experiment with a matched "just study it closely" control would separate the two.
