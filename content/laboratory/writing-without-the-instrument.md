## Question

Which writing capacities atrophy under assistance, and how quickly do they recover when it is removed?

## Input

A planned month of daily unassisted writing — 500 words minimum, no model, no autocomplete, no search beyond a dictionary — against a measured baseline taken in the week before.

## Method

*Planned. Not yet started.*

1. Baseline week: write normally, with whatever assistance is habitual. Keep every draft. At the end, record time-per-piece and note subjective difficulty in four areas: structure, word choice, holding the whole piece in mind, tolerating uncertainty.
2. Month of unassisted daily writing. Same log every day.
3. Re-test week: return to normal tools. Record whether anything feels different.

## Tools

A plain text editor with completion disabled. A paper dictionary. A timer. The log.

## Process

Not begun. Drafting the measurement rubric now; the four subjective areas are too vague to compare day to day.

## Result

None yet.

## Observations

None yet. One concern already: "no search beyond a dictionary" will make some pieces impossible to write honestly, so the topic choice will be constrained in a way that might matter.

## Failure points (anticipated)

- Subjective difficulty ratings are unreliable and drift.
- A month is long enough to improve simply through daily practice, independent of the assistance question.
- Motivation to keep the log honestly on day 19 is unproven.

## Next question

Will be set once there is a result. The likely candidates: does the recovery hold after tools return, and is 500 words a day enough load to see an effect at all?
