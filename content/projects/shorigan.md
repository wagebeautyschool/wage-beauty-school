## What Shorigan is

Shorigan began as a fictional world. It did not remain entirely fictional.

A philosophy developed alongside the story, and gradually became something that
could also be examined and applied outside of it. That creates a deliberate
uncertainty between imagination and reality: a principle can exist inside the
story, where it belongs to the mythology of Shorigan, and then be carried into
the real world, where its usefulness, its contradictions and its limits can be
tested. If an idea has value, that value does not need to depend on the world in
which it was invented.

## Two aspects

Shorigan is built in two halves that feed each other.

- **Living Philosophy** — the ideas themselves: recurring principles concerning
  attention, awareness, discipline, conviction, discernment, balance, action,
  acceptance, expression and uncertainty. They are not presented as commandments.
  They are instruments for examination. The tomes below are the current written
  form of this half.
- **Living Story** — the narrative work set in Shorigan, where those principles
  are first lived rather than argued. [Song of the Fallen](entry.html?c=projects&id=song-of-the-fallen)
  is the first published piece of it.

## Living Philosophy

The philosophy is not intended to function as a fixed canon. It is a living
system, and it holds to one rule above the rest:

> Reality comes first. Ideas are tools for understanding reality, not substitutes
> for it.

This matters because Shorigan is designed to resist the temptation to turn
philosophy into mythology simply because mythology makes philosophy feel
profound. The work is interested in consequence. What happens when a principle is
actually followed? What does it change? Where does it fail? What does it reveal?
What happens when two principles conflict — and what happens when an idea that
feels beautiful meets something real that does not agree with it? Those questions
are part of the work.

Shorigan is, in the end, an experiment in whether a philosophy can stay alive
while it moves between three spaces: imagination, language and reality. The story
provides the imagination. The philosophy provides the language. Reality provides
the test. Anything that survives that movement is worth keeping. Anything that
does not can be changed.
