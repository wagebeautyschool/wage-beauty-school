## Question

Can you run a task entirely through the instrument and still be the author of every decision in it?

## Context

Directing a powerful instrument well is a skill in its own right. It is not the same as typing a request and accepting what comes back. It looks more like editing: setting the intention, judging each return, sending it back with specific corrections, and knowing when to stop.

## Exercise

Pick a small, complete task — a page of writing, a simple script, a plan, a set of images. Do the whole thing with AI assistance, but hold yourself to one rule: **you must be able to explain every choice in the finished work as your choice.**

As you go, keep a running log:

- What did you ask for, and why that framing?
- What did it return that you kept? What did you reject, and on what grounds?
- Where did you override it? Where were you tempted to just accept something you had not really evaluated?
- How long did it take?

## Reflection

Look at the log. Count the decisions you actually made versus the ones you let through. The ratio is the real output of this exercise. Pair it with [Work Without AI](entry.html?c=practice&id=work-without-ai) and then [Compare the Two](entry.html?c=practice&id=compare-the-two).
