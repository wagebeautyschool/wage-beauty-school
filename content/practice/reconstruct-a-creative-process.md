## Question

What sequence of decisions produced the finished work — and where were the real forks?

## Context

We tend to experience good work as an object that simply exists. It is more useful to see it as the last frame of a long process of choices, most of which are invisible in the result. Reconstructing that process teaches you more than analysing the object does.

## Exercise

Pick a finished work you admire — an essay, a film scene, a piece of software, a song, a building. Write the plausible history of decisions behind it:

- What was the first version probably like?
- What are three things that were likely cut, and why?
- Where was there a genuine fork — a choice that could credibly have gone another way and changed the whole work?
- What constraint (time, money, medium, audience) shaped it most?
- What did the maker have to *decide to say* that no tool could have supplied?

You will be guessing. Guess specifically anyway.

## Reflection

Compare your reconstruction to how you actually work. Where do you skip a step the maker probably did not? Where do you agonise over a fork that probably did not matter?
