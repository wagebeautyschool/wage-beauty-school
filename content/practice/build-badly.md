## Question

What is the problem actually about — as opposed to what you assumed it was about?

## Context

A careful first attempt hides its assumptions inside good craft. A deliberately bad first attempt exposes them. When you build the crudest thing that could possibly work, the parts that break are the parts that matter, and they break loudly enough to see.

## Exercise

Take something you are about to make. Give yourself a hard, short time box — thirty minutes, an hour. Build the worst version that technically functions:

- ugly, unstyled, hard-coded, missing features
- no polish, no naming decisions, no cleanup
- just the skeleton doing the one thing

Then look at what fought you. Where did the crude version turn out to be genuinely hard? Where did it turn out to be trivial? Where did you discover the real question was different from the one you started with?

## Reflection

Write down the one thing the bad build taught you that a careful build would have buried. Keep the bad build until the real one exists. Then throw it away without ceremony.
