## Question

What is your main tool quietly deciding for you?

## Context

Every instrument has a grain — a direction it is easy to work in and directions it resists. A word processor wants paragraphs. A slide tool wants bullet points. A language model wants a confident, balanced, medium-length answer. You usually stop noticing the grain within a few days of using a tool, and after that it feels like neutral ground rather than a set of choices someone made.

## Exercise

Pick one tool you use for real work. Spend a single session using it normally, but keep a notepad beside you. Every time the tool makes something easy or hard, note it:

- What did it suggest before you asked?
- What was one click away, and what took real effort to reach?
- What did you *not* make because the tool made it awkward?
- Where did you accept its first offer without deciding to?

Do not change how you work during the session. You are observing, not reforming.

## Reflection

Afterwards, write a short paragraph: if a stranger only saw your output from that session, what would they assume you believe about the work — and how much of that was you, and how much was the grain of the tool?
